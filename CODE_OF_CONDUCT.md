# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue with the work, not the person.

Dibs invites criticism (including "this design is wrong") and that only works
if disagreement stays about the thing being built. Sharp technical pushback is
welcome and is not a conduct problem. Contempt for the person on the other end
of it is.

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Standards

Behaviour that helps:

- Being kind to people and rigorous about code
- Accepting that a well-argued disagreement is a contribution
- Saying "I was wrong about that" without ceremony
- Giving newcomers the context they are missing rather than the impression they
  should have known it

Behaviour that does not:

- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission
- Sustained disruption, or dismissing people rather than arguments

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces, issues, pull requests, discussions,
commits, and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behaviour privately through
[GitHub's report form](https://github.com/agenxy/dibs/security/advisories/new)
or by direct message to the maintainer. Reports are handled confidentially, and
you will get a response.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, and other
contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and will explain why when
they do.

Consequences escalate with severity and repetition: a private word, then a
warning, then a temporary ban, then a permanent one. Nobody is banned for being
wrong about a technical question, however loudly.
